A world that keeps opening up

An exploration sandbox, not a game with a score.

Earth and Solar Sim began as a quiet beach experience for VR. It grew into a small spherical world, then a miniature of the real Earth, and eventually a place to explore the solar system and nearby stars.

There is no required mission. Walk, swim, sail, drive, fly, take photographs, launch fireworks, or simply watch the light change. When you're ready, climb into a spacecraft and see how far you can go.

The real Earth, in miniature

A spherical world built from geographic data.

The home world is a miniature globe with real coastlines and elevation data, including mountains, rivers, lakes, deserts, and ice. Cities and airports are placed at their real locations, while procedural buildings and details make the landscape explorable.

From the ground, the horizon curves away. From orbit, continents and clouds come together into a globe. The atmosphere, changing daylight, weather, and shadows help connect the small world to the planet we know.

From the horizon to other worlds

Leave the beach behind and keep flying.

Fly above the atmosphere and the curved world becomes a planet beneath you. Continue outward to the Moon, Mars, an ice world, and a ringed gas giant. Each landable world has its own terrain, sky, lighting, and gravity.

The Moon and Mars use real elevation datasets as the basis for their landscapes, with additional craters and fine relief layered on top. You can visit notable locations on their surfaces through the teleport menu.

Real data, generated worlds. The home Earth and the Moon and Mars terrain draw on real geographic or elevation data. The surfaces of distant exoplanets are generated for exploration rather than presented as surveyed, real terrain.

Walk around your starship

A spacecraft is more than a cockpit.

Spawn a large starship, walk up its ramp, and explore its interior while it travels. Move through the cargo hold, engineering, crew spaces, lab, observation lounge, and bridge. The windows show the world outside, whether you're on the ground or in space.

You can take the helm, fly between worlds, land, and step back out to explore. A smaller spaceship and other vehicles offer different ways to move through the environment.

How it was built

One conversation, one request at a time.

Earth and Solar Sim began as Lantern Tide, a VR beach built around the ocean from Coastline, a coastal exploration project. A request for footprints in the sand led to a campfire, a lighthouse, swimming, islands, cities, a spherical world, real Earth data, other planets, and eventually a walkable starship.

The project was developed iteratively with Claude Code: requests became code changes, browser checks, screenshots, fixes, and new ideas. The history is part of the experience—a record of a small environment growing into something much larger.

Read the main development milestones

1.0–3.8 · The beach becomes an experience

The raft became a beach with footprints, sand drawing, a campfire, a lighthouse beam, weather, reefs, kelp, fish, a dock, a boat, swimming, and a VR menu.

4.0–6.2 · Islands and a curved world

Procedural islands brought cities, volcanoes, temples, swamps, roads, airports, and places to discover. The world was curved so the horizon could become part of the experience.

7.0–8.21 · A miniature Earth

The world became a true sphere with a fixed sun, a rotating planet, a moon with phases, real coastlines and elevation data, cities, airports, climate, weather, and a map for finding places.

8.22–8.30 · Vehicles, spacecraft, and nearby stars

A spawn menu added vehicles, wildlife, a camera and album, working cockpits, a walkable starship, faster space travel, and jumps to nearby star systems.

9.0–9.18 · Time, eclipses, moons, and real terrain

The project gained a selectable calendar, eclipse events checked against a catalogue, orbital modes, solar-system moons, an asteroid belt, rocks in Saturn's rings, and higher-resolution lunar and Martian terrain.

For the full version-by-version record, see VERSION.txt. For the design of the generated world and places of interest, see WORLD_DESIGN.md.

Under the hood

A large world assembled from compact rules.

  • Rendering: built with three.js, with custom shaders for water, terrain, sky, and atmospheric effects.
  • Streaming: nearby terrain is generated incrementally, with detail adjusted by distance.
  • Consistency: shared formulas help the land, water, and movement agree about the shape of the world.
  • Data and procedural content: real geographic and astronomical references are combined with generated buildings, terrain detail, and distant planetary surfaces.
